## What to study

### Vector spaces and subspaces

- [ ] Vector-space definitions
    - Real vector spaces and their axioms
    - Euclidean, matrix, polynomial and function-space examples
    - Subspace tests, intersections, sums and direct sums
    - Complex vector spaces after studying complex numbers
- [ ] Span, independence and bases
    - Linear combinations and spanning sets
    - Linear dependence and independence
    - Bases, dimension and coordinates
    - Basis extension and exchange theorems
    - Extending an independent set and extracting a basis
    - Dimension formula for sums and intersections
    - Quotient spaces and their dimensions
    - Coordinate changes and transition matrices

### The four fundamental subspaces

- [ ] Column, row, null and left-null spaces
    - Bases from elimination and careful use of the original columns
    - Rank, nullity and equality of row and column rank
    - Solvability of a system through its column space
    - Dimensions and the rank-nullity relation

### Linear maps and matrix representations

- [ ] Structure-preserving maps
    - Linearity tests and geometric transformations
    - Kernel, image, injectivity and surjectivity
    - Rank-nullity theorem for linear maps
    - Composition, inverses and isomorphisms
- [ ] Matrices relative to bases
    - Representing maps between different vector spaces
    - Change of basis for maps and operators
    - Similarity of matrices representing the same operator
    - Dual spaces, dual bases and linear functionals
    - Dual maps, annihilators and the finite-dimensional double dual

### Reasoning about linear structure

- [ ] Proofs and counterexamples in linear algebra
    - Direct proof from the axioms and proof by cases
    - Contrapositive and contradiction in independence and invertibility arguments
    - Necessary and sufficient conditions, converses and counterexamples
    - Proving existence and uniqueness separately

### Further examples

- [ ] Linear structure beyond coordinate vectors
    - Evaluation, differentiation and integration as maps on suitable spaces
    - Linear constraints, affine solution sets and convex combinations
    - Finite-dimensional reasoning and the limits of its extension to infinite dimensions

## Reading

Lang, Chapters III-V, including the change-of-bases appendix; Strang, Chapter 2. Connect every abstract definition to a small matrix or polynomial example.
