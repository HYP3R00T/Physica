Classical mechanics begins with quantities we can measure: position, time and mass. From these, we build descriptions of motion and examine how interactions change it. The same ideas help us understand a falling object, a rotating wheel or a system of interacting particles.

This path moves from measurement and kinematics through Newton's laws, energy and momentum, then into rotational motion and more advanced formulations of mechanics. Familiar models give the ideas something concrete to work with; later methods let us describe more complicated systems.
