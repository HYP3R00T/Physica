## Angles and orthogonality

For nonzero Euclidean vectors, the angle $\theta\in[0,\pi]$ satisfies

$$
\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=\|\mathbf u\|\,\|\mathbf v\|\cos\theta,
\qquad
\cos\theta=\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf u\|\,\|\mathbf v\|}
$$

The Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

$$
|\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v|\leq\|\mathbf u\|\,\|\mathbf v\|,
$$

ensures that the ratio lies between $-1$ and $1$. Equality holds exactly when the vectors are linearly dependent. One proof, for $\mathbf v\ne\mathbf0$, expands the nonnegative quantity

$$
\left\|\mathbf u-\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf v\|^2}\mathbf v\right\|^2
=\|\mathbf u\|^2-\frac{(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v)^2}{\|\mathbf v\|^2}
$$

Cauchy–Schwarz also gives the triangle inequality by expanding $\|\mathbf u+\mathbf v\|^2$.

Vectors are orthogonal when $\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=0$. For two nonzero vectors, this means they are perpendicular. The zero vector is orthogonal to every vector, although it has no geometric direction or angle.

## Projections

<Definition title="Projection onto a direction">

For a nonzero vector $\mathbf v$, the scalar projection of $\mathbf u$ onto its direction and the vector projection onto its span are

$$
\operatorname{comp}_{\mathbf v}\mathbf u
=\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf v\|},
\qquad
\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf v}\mathbf u
=\frac{\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v}{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v}\mathbf v
$$

</Definition>

The scalar projection is a signed length. The vector projection lies along $\mathbf v$. The remaining vector $\mathbf u-\operatorname{proj}_{\mathbf v}\mathbf u$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf v$.

For $\mathbf u=(2,3)$ and $\mathbf v=(1,0)$, the projection is $(2,0)$ and the remainder is $(0,3)$. Resolving a vector into mutually perpendicular unit directions uses these dot products as coefficients. With nonparallel directions that are not perpendicular, coefficients must instead be found by solving the component equations.

## Direction angles

In $\mathbb R^3$, the angles $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ with the positive coordinate axes obey

$$
\cos\alpha=\frac{v_1}{\|\mathbf v\|},\quad
\cos\beta=\frac{v_2}{\|\mathbf v\|},\quad
\cos\gamma=\frac{v_3}{\|\mathbf v\|}
$$

Their squared cosines sum to $1$. In the plane, the direction angle of $(x,y)\ne(0,0)$ is obtained with $\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)$, which uses both signs to select the quadrant. Using only $\arctan(y/x)$ loses that information and fails when $x=0$.
