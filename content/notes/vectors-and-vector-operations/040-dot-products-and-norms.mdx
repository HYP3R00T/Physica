## Dot product

<Definition title="Euclidean dot product">

For $\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^n$, their **dot product**, also called their scalar product, is

$$
\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_iv_i
$$

Its result is a real scalar.

</Definition>

For example, $(2,3)\cdot(4,-1)=8-3=5$. The dot product is symmetric and linear in each argument:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf v&=\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf u,\\
(a\mathbf u+b\mathbf v)\cdot\mathbf w
&=a(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf w)+b(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf w)
\end{aligned}
$$

Also, $\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v\geq0$, with equality exactly when $\mathbf v=\mathbf0$.

## Euclidean norm

<Definition title="Euclidean norm">

The **Euclidean norm** of $\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^n$ is

$$
\|\mathbf v\|=\sqrt{\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf v}
=\sqrt{v_1^2+\cdots+v_n^2}
$$

</Definition>

This is its length or magnitude in Euclidean geometry. For example, $\|(3,4)\|=5$. The formula extends the Pythagorean theorem to $n$ coordinates.

A norm measures size. The Euclidean norm obeys

$$
\|\mathbf v\|\geq0,\qquad
\|\mathbf v\|=0\iff\mathbf v=\mathbf0,\qquad
\|a\mathbf v\|=|a|\,\|\mathbf v\|,
$$

and the triangle inequality,

$$
\|\mathbf u+\mathbf v\|\leq\|\mathbf u\|+\|\mathbf v\|
$$

For points with position vectors $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$, the Euclidean distance is $\|\mathbf b-\mathbf a\|$.

## Unit vectors and normalization

A unit vector has norm $1$. To normalize a nonzero vector, divide it by its length:

$$
\widehat{\mathbf v}=\frac{\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf v\|}
$$

Then $\|\widehat{\mathbf v}\|=1$, and it points in the same direction as $\mathbf v$. For example, normalizing $(3,4)$ gives $(3/5,4/5)$. The zero vector cannot be normalized because its norm is zero.

A vector space alone does not specify a norm or a dot product. Here we have chosen the usual Euclidean ones on $\mathbb R^n$.
