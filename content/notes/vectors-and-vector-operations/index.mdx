Begin with coordinate vectors and their representations. Then study operations and linear combinations before using dot products to describe length, angles and projections. Lines and planes give concrete examples before the real vector-space axioms generalize the algebraic structure.

## Reading order

1. [Vectors](/vectors): coordinate definition, notation and geometric interpretation.
2. [Vector operations](/vector-operations): addition, subtraction and scalar multiplication.
3. [Linear combinations](/linear-combinations): span and components along chosen directions.
4. [Dot products and norms](/dot-products-and-norms): length, distance and normalization.
5. [Angles and projections](/angles-and-projections): orthogonality and vector resolution.
6. [Vector equations of lines and planes](/vector-lines-and-planes): parameters, normals, intersections and distances.
7. [Real vector spaces](/real-vector-spaces): the axioms and the general meaning of a vector.
8. [Vector independence and bases](/vector-independence-and-bases): dependence, unique coordinates and dimension.
9. [Cross and triple products](/cross-and-triple-products): areas and volumes in three dimensions.

## Reading

Serge Lang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra*, second edition, Chapter I, is the primary reading for coordinate vectors. The real vector-space definition provides a bridge to Chapter III. The notes follow the topic order above; the chapter order need not match it exactly.

[Interactive Linear Algebra](https://textbooks.math.gatech.edu/ila/vectors.html) offers additional coordinate examples. [Ximera's vector-space definition](https://ximera.osu.edu/linearalgebra/textbook/vectorSpaces/definition) supports the transition to abstract vectors.
