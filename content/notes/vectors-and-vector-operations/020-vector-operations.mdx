## Addition and scalar multiplication

<Definition title="Coordinate operations">

For $\mathbf u,\mathbf v\in\mathbb R^n$ and $a\in\mathbb R$,

$$
\mathbf u+\mathbf v=(u_1+v_1,\ldots,u_n+v_n),
\qquad
 a\mathbf v=(av_1,\ldots,av_n)
$$

</Definition>

For example, $(2,3)+(-1,4)=(1,7)$ and $2(2,3)=(4,6)$. Addition combines displacements: place the second arrow at the endpoint of the first. The resultant arrow runs from the initial point to the final point. This is the head-to-tail or triangle construction. Drawing both arrows from a common point gives the parallelogram construction. Repeating head-to-tail addition gives the polygon construction for several vectors.

A positive scalar preserves the direction of a nonzero vector; a negative scalar reverses it. Multiplication by zero gives the zero vector. The magnitude is multiplied by $|a|$.

## Zero, negatives and subtraction

The zero vector is $\mathbf0=(0,\ldots,0)$. It has no direction. The opposite vector is $-\mathbf v=(-v_1,\ldots,-v_n)$, and

$$
\mathbf v+(-\mathbf v)=\mathbf0,
\qquad
\mathbf u-\mathbf v=\mathbf u+(-\mathbf v)
$$

For nonzero vectors, $\mathbf u=a\mathbf v$ means they are parallel: they have the same direction for $a>0$ and opposite directions for $a<0$. Opposite-direction vectors are called antiparallel.

## Algebraic properties

Component arithmetic gives, for all appropriate vectors and real scalars,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf u+\mathbf v&=\mathbf v+\mathbf u,\\
(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)+\mathbf w&=\mathbf u+(\mathbf v+\mathbf w),\\
a(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)&=a\mathbf u+a\mathbf v,\\
(a+b)\mathbf v&=a\mathbf v+b\mathbf v,\\
a(b\mathbf v)&=(ab)\mathbf v,\\
1\mathbf v&=\mathbf v
\end{aligned}
$$

For instance, the $i$th component of $a(\mathbf u+\mathbf v)$ is $a(u_i+v_i)=au_i+av_i$, proving distributivity in every component. These are the properties we will later collect in the vector-space definition.
