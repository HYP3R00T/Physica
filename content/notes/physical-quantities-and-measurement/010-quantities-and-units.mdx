## A quantity and its numerical value

So when we say 'quantity' we commenly understand it as the question **How much** of something and it quite precisely is exactly that. The word **How** is a basic and important aspect of any scientific principle not just physics and its basics but everything and it shows up here as well. When we define the 'quantity' of something we are asking how can we invision it ourselves ?, How an u know something without knowing IT. soo precisely thats just what quantities are.

Let's suppose you are measuring your height and u take a measuring tape and use it to measure yourself, here you will get 2 things from that tape in different variations depending on the tape but all of them mean the same. First you will get a number, lets say 170 or 5 which are numbers and the more important factor a letter after that number which will likely say either cm or feet. Here 170 means the numerical value of your height and the cm or feet define how much that number means. for example 1 feet might be the length of your whole hand but 1 cm will be a much smaller than than even your individual fingers.

Here that **Number** is the numerical physical quantity of your height and that **Letter** is what define's how long it would be in real life. This letter is what we call a unit and they are used in different ways depending on what we are measuring and where it is being used. Everything has a measurement and in consequence a measurement unit that is used to define that measurement.

Writing the length as $120\,\mathrm{cm}$ gives the number a reference. The centimetre is the unit, and 120 is the numerical value in that unit.

### In scientific terms

A physical quantity is a property that can be measured or expressed quantitatively. Length, mass and time are examples. For a length, we can write:

$$
\ell = 120\,\mathrm{cm} %= 1.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The table has not changed. We have changed the unit used to express its length, so the numerical value changes with it. A larger unit requires a smaller numerical value for the same quantity.

## Converting units

One useful and so fun idea about units is that we have divided and differenciated different units from each other so we can convert them. Lets say from the earlier example where you measured your height now you want to a curtain for your house and u measured the hight that u want the curtain to be using the same measuring tape from before where you got 120cm but when you go check amazon the curtains are labeled based on 'meter' instead of centimeter?

Here the measurement is still length/height of the curtain but this time we are using a bigger measurement just to make it easier for yourselves soo if there is a 100centimeter long curtain then we can just call it a 1 meter. Then if we want to convert them we can using simple math given below:

### In mathematical terms

Since one metre equals one hundred centimetres, the ratio below equals one. Multiplying by it changes the expression without changing the length:

$$
\\100\,\mathrm{cm} = 1\,\mathrm{m}
\\or,\ 1\,\mathrm{cm} = \frac{1\,\mathrm{m}}{100\,\mathrm{cm}}
$$

**Multiplying both sides by 120cm,**

$$
or,\ 120\,\mathrm{cm}\times\ 1\,\mathrm{cm} = 120\,\mathrm{cm}\times\frac{1\,\mathrm{m}}{100\,\mathrm{cm}}
\\or,\ 120\,\mathrm{cm} = 120\,\mathrm{cm}\times\frac{1\,\mathrm{m}}{100\,\mathrm{cm}} = 1.20\,\mathrm{m}
$$

The centimetres cancel, leaving metres. Writing the units throughout the calculation helps us choose the correct conversion factor.

<Callout title="Question" color="blue">

If the numerical value changes when we change units, what stays the same?

</Callout>

The physical quantity stays the same. Both expressions describe the same length. This distinction matters whenever we compare measurements or substitute values into an equation.

## Base and derived quantities

As discussed above, Quantities of stuff is just the measurement of 'how much' of something there is and soo to further elaborate on that point on quantities there are mainly 2 types of quantities.

Base quantities or also known as fundamental quantities are quantities that are singular and self defining as in they donot comprise of any other form of measurement and are the fundamental mode of measurement in their measuring quantity such as meter, second, kilogram, centimeter, etc all exactly define one thing be that distance, time, weight, etc

And opposite to Base quantities are derived quantities that are comprised of multiple base quantities and are used to define more complex phenomenons like as velocity(m/s), area($$ {m}^2$$), etc where velocity for example is written as meter/second which means to indicate how many meter's something moved in the time of second's.
