# Mathematics coverage review

The foundation uses the mathematics section of the [JEE Advanced 2026 syllabus](https://jeeadv.ac.in/documents/jee-advanced-2026-syllabus.pdf), pages 9–12. The list below follows its subject order. Files live in `content/roadmap/mathematics/`, grouped visually into mathematical strands. The website graph uses prerequisites instead, so parallel subjects may appear in a different order.

This review checks the named syllabus areas and preserves the mathematical material in the previous roadmap. It is a working curriculum for physics, not a claim to cover every undergraduate or postgraduate mathematics course.

## Foundation

1. Sets, relations and functions
   - Set representations, operations and De Morgan's laws
   - Cartesian products, relations and equivalence relations
   - Function types, domains, composition, inverses and graphs
   - Elementary function families introduced by name; their calculations continue in algebra and trigonometry
2. Algebra
   - Expressions, polynomials, equations, inequalities and logarithms
   - Complex arithmetic, conjugation, modulus and polynomial roots
   - Progressions, finite sums, geometric series, counting and the binomial theorem
   - Complex arguments, polar forms and roots of unity continue after trigonometry
3. Matrices and determinants
   - Operations, transposes, special matrices and determinants
   - Row and column transformations, adjugates, inverses and linear systems
   - Vector spaces and eigenvalue methods continue in linear algebra
4. Probability and statistics
   - Events, probability rules, conditioning, independence and Bayes' theorem
   - Counting, discrete random variables, expectation and variance
   - Frequency distributions, central tendency and dispersion
   - Continuous distributions, joint distributions and inference continue at university level
5. Trigonometry
   - Functions, graphs, periodicity and equations
   - Addition, multiple-angle, half-angle and inverse-function identities
   - Radian measure, triangle relations and small-angle approximations support physics
6. Analytical geometry
   - Plane coordinates, lines, triangle centres and circles
   - Conics, parametric equations, tangents, normals and loci
   - Spatial lines and planes, distances, angles and coplanarity
   - Polar coordinates added for physics
7. Differential calculus
   - Limits, continuity, differentiation and mean value theorems
   - Implicit and parametric derivatives, extrema and graph behaviour
   - Rates, motion and approximation connect these methods to physics
8. Integral calculus
   - Antiderivatives, definite integrals, their properties and the fundamental theorem
   - Substitution, integration by parts and partial fractions
   - Areas, accumulation and selected physical applications
   - Improper integrals retained from the earlier roadmap
   - The syllabus's first-order differential equations have their own following segment, including homogeneous substitutions and integrating factors
9. Vectors
   - Components, arithmetic, projections, dot and cross products
   - Scalar and vector triple products and geometric interpretations
   - Coordinate rotations retained for physics

## Deliberate scope choices

The syllabus includes constructing families of circles through prescribed intersection points and lines through the intersection of other lines. Basic intersection methods are included; these specialised construction families are not separate requirements. They can be added if a physics topic needs them. No other named syllabus family was deliberately dropped; related items are grouped under searchable topic names rather than reproduced sentence by sentence.

Complex polar notation is placed after trigonometry to avoid using an unexplained angle representation in algebra. Elementary probability is independent of calculus. Introductory matrices do not require vector spaces. Basic function recognition uses Class 10 algebra; further manipulation belongs to the algebra segment.

## Beyond the foundation

- Complex numbers and complex exponentials
  - Polar forms, roots, amplitudes and logarithms
- Linear algebra
  - Vector spaces, linear maps, inner products, eigenvalues and quadratic forms
- Introductory and linear differential equations
  - First-order methods, second-order equations, coupled systems and series solutions
- Sequences, series and approximations
  - Convergence tests, Taylor expansions and error estimates
- Multivariable calculus
  - Partial derivatives, multiple integrals, constraints and Legendre transformations
- Calculus of variations
  - Functionals, stationary action, boundary terms and field variations
- Vector calculus and fields
  - Differential operators, integral theorems and potentials
- Probability distributions and statistical methods
  - Continuous and joint distributions, limits and inference
- Complex analysis
  - Analytic functions, contour methods, residues and branches
- Fourier and Laplace transforms
  - Series, transforms, distributions and convolution
- Special functions
  - Orthogonal polynomials and common functions used in physical equations
- Partial differential equations and boundary-value problems
  - Separation of variables, Sturm–Liouville theory and Green's functions
- Hilbert spaces and operators
  - Function spaces, domains, spectra and tensor products
- Tensors
  - Transformation laws, contractions and metrics
- Groups and symmetry
  - Representations, Lie groups and physical symmetry groups
- Asymptotic and perturbation methods
  - Stationary phase, saddle points and perturbation expansions
- Differential geometry for gravitation
  - Manifolds, connections, geodesics and curvature

Numerical methods remain in the methods strand. Nonlinear dynamics remains in mechanics. Their placement does not exclude them from the mathematical tools used in physics.

## Reference cross-checks

The locally available book contents were used to check subject boundaries and standard terminology:

- OpenStax, Calculus, volumes 1–3: single-variable calculus, series, differential equations, spatial geometry and multivariable calculus
- Serge Lang, Introduction to Linear Algebra, second edition: matrices, vector spaces, linear maps, inner products and eigenvalues
- Arfken and Weber, Mathematical Methods for Physicists, sixth edition: vector analysis, tensors, groups, complex analysis, differential equations, special functions, transforms and probability

These are coverage and terminology checks, not a chapter-by-chapter reading assignment or a complete audit of every book. New segments should still be reviewed individually before publication.
