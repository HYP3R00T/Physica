# Thermal and statistical physics curriculum

Proposed on 15 September 2026. Ten segments are visible for author review, with topic checklists and no outcome sections. These files are a first full draft, not a completed source audit.

## Proposed sequence

- Thermal equilibrium and equations of state
    - Macroscopic states, temperature, material response and gas models.
- Heat, work and the first law
    - Energy transfers, heat capacities, enthalpy and thermodynamic processes.
- Entropy and the second law
    - Reversibility, engine limits, entropy balances and the third law.
- Thermodynamic potentials and equilibrium
    - Natural variables, response identities, stability, coexistence and mixtures.
- Kinetic theory and transport
    - Molecular distributions, collisions, kinetic equations and heat transfer.
- Statistical description and microcanonical equilibrium
    - State counting, probability, statistical entropy and isolated-system equilibrium.
- Ensembles and classical statistical mechanics
    - Reservoirs, partition functions, interactions, fluctuations and sampling.
- Quantum statistics and quantum gases
    - Quantum ensembles, Bose and Fermi gases, radiation and lattice heat capacities.
- Phase transitions and critical phenomena
    - Collective order, statistical models, scaling and introductory renormalisation.
- Nonequilibrium statistical physics
    - Irreversible transport, stochastic dynamics, response and relaxation.

The first four segments develop macroscopic thermodynamics. Kinetic theory supplies a molecular model before the general statistical framework. The later segments broaden that framework to quantum particles, collective behaviour and evolution away from equilibrium. Advanced topics labelled as introductions or overviews mark the boundary of this shared route.

Phase coexistence appears first as a thermodynamic equilibrium condition. It returns later through microscopic models, order parameters and critical fluctuations. Likewise, the early transport laws precede their kinetic and statistical explanations. These returns serve different purposes.

## Graph and prerequisites

The strand branches from Work and energy. Each subsequent segment lists the preceding thermal segment as a cumulative reading requirement. This preserves the current curriculum convention without claiming that every previous model is needed for every later calculation.

Kinetic theory also requires Impulse, momentum and collisions. The statistical-foundations segment adds Hamiltonian mechanics for phase-space measures and Liouville's theorem. These independent requirements are not repeated downstream.

The graph still needs foundations that have not yet been authored:

- Multivariable calculus and partial-derivative identities for thermodynamics; Legendre transformations, constrained extrema and stability analysis for potentials.
- Probability, combinatorics, asymptotics and distributions for kinetic theory and statistical mechanics.
- Partial differential equations and continuum balances for transport; molecular models beyond elastic collisions where needed.
- Quantum states, operators, identical-particle symmetry and quantised oscillators before quantum statistics. The checklist names their statistical use but cannot replace a quantum-mechanics foundation.
- Magnetic interactions before treating the Ising model as a microscopic theory of a material. It can initially be studied as an abstract statistical model.
- Fourier methods, field descriptions and scaling mathematics for critical phenomena; stochastic processes and numerical methods for nonequilibrium physics.

Existing acoustic segments retain their approved dependencies. Their thermal links should be added after this strand is reviewed, at the relevant first use of compressibility, constitutive response or thermal transport. Do not treat an absent link as evidence that the foundation is unnecessary.

All segments use the existing `Core` scope for the proposed shared route. They do not have equal difficulty: the final sections include introductory graduate material. Full quantum criticality, interacting quantum fluids, specialised condensed-matter models, chemical engineering, turbulence and far-from-equilibrium field theories remain possible continuations.

## Initial scope reference

The [IIT Kanpur Physics course booklet](https://iitk.ac.in/phy/data/PHY-CourseBooklet-18-03-24.pdf), retrieved with a 2026 cover, provides an initial comparison: PHY211 for thermal physics, PHY412 for statistical mechanics, and PHY613/PHY615 for advanced critical and nonequilibrium directions. The inspected entries distinguish introductory thermodynamics and ensembles from more specialised material. Their topics inform the proposed range; their course boundaries are not copied as segment boundaries.

A multi-book and multi-institution audit remains to be done after author review. The present draft does not claim to match the author's SVNIT electives or to cover every specialisation.
