# Relativity and gravitation curriculum

Proposed on 16 September 2026. Ten segments are visible for author review, with `draft: false`. This is the initial topic-list draft, not the subsequent multi-book audit. The author approved committing this baseline on 16 September 2026. The detailed source audit remains pending.

## Proposed sequence

- Special relativity and spacetime
- Relativistic particle dynamics
- Relativistic fields and stress-energy
- Equivalence principle and curved spacetime
- Einstein field equations and gravitational dynamics
- Spherical gravity and relativistic stars
- Black holes and horizons
- Weak gravity and gravitational radiation
- Relativistic cosmology
- Gravitational evolution and global spacetime structure

The first three segments establish special relativity and relativistic descriptions of matter. The next two develop geometry and gravitational field equations. Spherical bodies, black holes, radiation and cosmology provide substantial physical models. The final segment introduces evolution and global questions needed for advanced general relativity.

`Core` means the shared route through this strand. It does not mean the whole route is elementary. Tensor geometry, gravitational radiation and global evolution require preparation beyond introductory mechanics.

## Dependencies and earlier coverage

The first segment begins at Kinematics. Relativistic particle dynamics adds Impulse, momentum and collisions, so relativistic conservation is developed after the corresponding Newtonian concepts. Each later segment depends on its immediate predecessor, following the agreed cumulative reading trail.

- Relativistic fields adds Maxwell's equations and field conservation for the covariant electromagnetic example. It does not depend on the existing Relativistic electrodynamics segment.
- Curved spacetime adds Gravitation and central-force motion for Newtonian gravity, non-inertial frames and tidal comparisons.
- Einstein field equations adds Lagrangian mechanics for variational formulations.
- Relativistic stars adds Thermal equilibrium and equations of state. Microscopic stellar equations of state remain a later material-physics requirement.
- Gravitational evolution adds Hamiltonian mechanics for the canonical formulation.

No redundant inherited prerequisite links are intended. These are reading-order dependencies, not a claim that every preceding model is essential to every later calculation.

Existing Relativistic electrodynamics already introduces Lorentz transformations and covariant fields. This draft gives those foundations a dedicated route and retains the electromagnetic example for continuity. Reviewing overlap and linking the older segment should happen after author inspection. The first two new segments can also supply the relativity assumed in quantum phenomena; those reverse links have not been added during this draft.

## Scope decisions

Classical mechanics retains Newtonian gravitation and central-force orbits. This strand develops relativistic geometry and dynamics instead of moving those approved topics.

Spherical gravity groups the exterior solution, orbital tests and stellar interiors. Black holes then focuses on horizons, rotation and strong-field causal structure. Weak gravity and radiation develops perturbative methods and signals. This order compares exact and approximate methods without creating a separate segment for each named spacetime.

Cosmology covers relativistic expansion, observational geometry and the starting point for perturbations. Detailed thermal history, nucleosynthesis, recombination, inflationary field models and structure formation can grow into a cosmology strand. Their full coverage is not claimed here.

Black-hole mechanics is classical. Hawking radiation, the quantum interpretation of entropy and semiclassical backreaction require quantum-field foundations. Relativistic quantum mechanics also remains a separate continuation; it is not supplied by classical spacetime dynamics.

The final segment introduces the initial-value problem, causal structure and asymptotic charges. Full numerical relativity, mathematical proofs of global theorems, modified gravity and quantum gravity remain advanced continuations.

## Missing foundations

- Special relativity assumes algebra, coordinate geometry and hyperbolic functions. Existing vector operations do not supply general tensor algebra.
- Curved-spacetime calculations need multivariable calculus, linear algebra, differential geometry and differential equations. The physics lists identify their uses, but dedicated mathematical segments remain necessary.
- Radiation methods require partial differential equations, Green functions, Fourier analysis and asymptotic expansions. The existing wave strand provides physical context but not all of that mathematics.
- Relativistic stellar models need microphysical equations of state and stability methods beyond the introductory thermal prerequisite.
- Global evolution requires hypersurface geometry and hyperbolic differential equations. Numerical methods and error control need further study modules.
- Links from electromagnetism and quantum mechanics must be checked for cycles and overly broad inherited requirements before being added.

## Initial course comparison

[MIT 8.962 lecture summaries, spring 2020](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-962-general-relativity-spring-2020/pages/lecture-summaries/) provide an initial comparison for the progression from tensors to curvature and field equations, followed by radiation, cosmology and compact sources. Its advanced summaries also introduce post-Newtonian and numerical methods. Our grouping uses different boundaries and orders the physical models differently.

The [MIT syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-962-general-relativity-spring-2020/pages/syllabus/) identifies this as a graduate course and lists mathematical preparation. This reinforces the need to author supporting mathematics rather than label the full sequence as beginner material.

This comparison establishes an initial scope reference, not a comprehensive audit. The next review should inspect local relativity textbooks and several institutional courses, check the special-relativity foundation separately, and examine the depth of the global endpoint.
