# Atomic and molecular physics curriculum

Current grouping: see [Segment consolidation](roadmap-consolidation.md) and the [roadmap index](roadmap-index.md). Original drafting counts and audit descriptions below record the earlier file boundaries.

Proposed on 16 September 2026. Ten segments are visible with `draft: false` for author review. This is the initial draft; the detailed textbook and institutional audit remains pending. Baseline approved for commit on 17 September 2026.

## Proposed sequence

- Atomic structure spectra and external fields
- Molecular structure spectra and dynamics
- Coherent light-matter interactions
- Atomic collisions cooling and trapping
- Precision atomic and molecular measurements

The route develops atomic states and spectra, molecular structure and motion, then interactions and experimental control. It uses the methods introduced in Quantum mechanics to describe particular physical systems. Segments now use Foundations or Advanced relative to this strand; see [Segment stages](roadmap-stages.md).

## Dependencies and overlap

Atomic structure begins at Composite quantum systems and identical particles. That path already supplies angular momentum, stationary approximations, transition theory and elementary molecular models. Each later segment depends on its immediate predecessor as the agreed cumulative reading trail.

Coherent light-matter interactions adds Quantum measurement and open systems for reduced dynamics. Its inherited quantum path also supplies scattering theory before the collision segment, so another direct scattering link would be redundant. Cooling adds Kinetic theory and transport for velocity distributions, collision rates and rethermalisation.

Hydrogen, helium, molecular bonding and driven two-state systems already occur as models in Quantum mechanics. Here they support configuration assignments, resolved spectra, molecular state classification and experimental response. The intent is to develop those uses rather than repeat general derivations of the same operators.

Optics already includes instruments, laser operation and spectral detection. This strand concentrates on the atoms and molecules being probed or controlled. Existing optical and thermal segments were not changed or linked back during this draft. Those links need a separate scope and cycle review.

The external-field segment introduces molecular dipoles and rotor response qualitatively. The later molecular segments develop electronic structure and rotational spectra in detail. Review this small forward introduction rather than treating it as a requirement to know the complete molecular spectrum beforehand.

## Scope boundaries

Electronic-structure methods are introduced at the level needed to interpret atomic and molecular states. Detailed quantum chemistry, density-functional development and large-scale electronic-structure computation can continue separately.

Light-matter coherence and elementary cavity coupling belong here as physical descriptions of driven emitters. Full photon statistics, field-state engineering and quantum communication belong to a later quantum-optics or information route.

Cooling ends at preparation, scattering control and the onset of quantum degeneracy. Equations of state, interacting quantum gases and collective phases connect to statistical and condensed-matter physics. This segment does not attempt to replace those strands.

Lamb shifts, heavy-atom corrections and precision symmetry tests identify where relativistic theory and quantum electrodynamics are needed. Their full derivations remain outside this draft. Nuclear moments and sizes are used as atomic inputs; nuclear structure requires its own treatment.

## Missing foundations and review questions

- Atomic terms and molecular symmetry require group representations, tensor operators and matrix methods. The quantum strand supplies physical introductions, but complete mathematical prerequisites remain missing.
- Molecular normal modes require multivariable calculus, eigenvalue methods and symmetry analysis. General molecular point groups and character tables need supporting mathematical coverage.
- Collision models require multichannel differential equations, continuum normalisation and numerical convergence methods. Velocity averaging assumes probability and kinetic theory.
- Precision measurements require statistics, estimation, noise spectra and uncertainty propagation beyond a list of instrument names.
- Resonator and frequency-comb methods need supporting optics. Decide which existing optical segments provide useful prerequisites without forcing every earlier optical topic onto this route.
- Detailed radiative corrections need relativistic quantum theory and field quantisation beyond the existing introductory treatment. Classical special relativity alone does not supply them.
- Review the depth of polyatomic dynamics, ultracold preparation and precision measurements during the source audit. These are proposed endpoints, not a claim that one introductory course contains all of them.

## Initial course comparison

[MIT 8.421 Atomic and Optical Physics I, spring 2014](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-421-atomic-and-optical-physics-i-spring-2014/pages/syllabus/) provides a comparison for atomic structure, external fields, line shapes and coherent radiation interactions. It is a graduate course, so its breadth is not used to label these topics as elementary.

[MIT 5.80 Small-Molecule Spectroscopy and Dynamics, fall 2008](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-80-small-molecule-spectroscopy-and-dynamics-fall-2008/pages/lecture-notes/) connects electronic states, Born–Oppenheimer separation, rovibrational coupling and spectral assignments. It supports giving molecular structure and dynamics more space than a short bonding overview.

[MIT 8.422 Atomic and Optical Physics II, spring 2013](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-422-atomic-and-optical-physics-ii-spring-2013/pages/syllabus/) provides an initial advanced comparison for light forces, cooling, trapping and collisions. Our ten boundaries are a proposed organisation, not a reproduction of any one course.

A later audit should compare local atomic and molecular textbooks and additional institutional courses, then record omissions, misplaced topics and specialist material to defer. This initial comparison does not replace that audit.
